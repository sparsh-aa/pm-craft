# Growth

## First question: "How does the product grow?"
Ask five people to whiteboard it. Different, partial or output-only ($) answers mean the team can't prioritise
coherently. The answer should be a **loop**, not a funnel.

## AARRR and why funnels aren't enough
Acquisition → Activation → Retention → Referral → Revenue (McClure). Use funnels to optimise a *step*. Funnels used
as the company model cause three problems: strategic silos (product vs channel vs monetisation planned separately),
functional silos (marketing floods low-quality signups to hit its number, which tanks retention), and one direction
(no reinvestment, so growth needs ever more input).

## Growth loops (Reforge)
Loop = input → action → output that is reinvested as the next input. Lesson shorthand: trigger → action → reward →
loop completion.
- **Content/UGC loop**: Pinterest: signup → save/repin (quality signal) → boards indexed by Google → searcher signs up. Grubhub geo × cuisine pages. Instagram posts.
- **Viral loop**: Dropbox give/get storage. Product must get better when friends join; that is hard (works for Slack, Snapchat; didn't for Pinterest).
- **Paid loop**: revenue → ads → users. Easy to start, but masks retention problems. Without a network effect it is "a race to the bottom" (worse customers at higher CAC). Premature scaling kills.
- **Sales loop**: contract profit → more reps. Needs high LTV/ACV.
- **Engagement loop**: Duolingo lessons → progress/streak → return.
Fast growers run on 1–2 strong loops that shift over time. Measure loop health and work on the **slowest part of the
loop**. Prefer compounding: 20 users growing 10% a week beats 500 users once. Organise cross-functional teams around a
loop's output.
Three sustainability questions (Winters): Can we retain users who find value? Can we monetise them? Does that support
a sustainable acquisition loop?

## Balfour's Four Fits ($100M+)
- **Market–Product**: start from the market and its problem, not the product.
- **Product–Channel**: "Products are built to fit channels; channels don't mold to products."
- **Channel–Model**: the ARPU ↔ CAC spectrum. Low ARPU needs near-free channels (virality, SEO, UGC). High ARPU can afford sales.
- **Model–Market**: ARPU × customers in market × % capturable ≥ target.
All four are needed, they interlock, and they shift, especially when moving up or down market.

## Channels that actually scale (Chen)
Paid (keep LTV:CAC ≈ 3:1), virality (K > 1), SEO (needs masses of unique content), sales (reps must return a revenue
multiple), and rare partnerships. They scale because they are feedback loops with high ceilings.
Early on, do unscalable things: friends, communities, niche blogs, cold email, contests, niche press. 5–7% a week is
good (PG). **Barbell**: run low-risk unscalable tactics for momentum while deliberately building toward one scalable
channel. Kindle strategy ≠ fire strategy (SEO is fire, not kindle).

## Viral loop design (Chen)
Viral loop = the steps from a user entering to inviting the next users.
- Choose the viral medium (integration ease × response rate: WhatsApp, feed, email).
- Keep the funnel to 2–3 screens. Registration can lose 80–90%. Progressive commitment.
- Build the hook into the core product: self-expression or communication.
- Add on-ramps: homepage, paid, SEO.
Stages: strategise → build fast and throwaway → trickle paid traffic → optimise (shorten flow, emphasise the next
step, test value props, more invitees, no user-hostile tricks) → polish and spend "excess" virality on retention.
If optimisation stalls, rework the value prop. Generic recipes decay. Loops tied to the core product last.

## Referral programs (Chen, ran Uber referrals)
Referrals hand your CAC to users. A good program is 10–30% of acquisition. They work where word of mouth already
exists and LTV is high (Dropbox: 100K → 4M users in 15 months, after paid CAC of $233–388 on a $99 product). They are
weak for low-LTV free apps and fade as the market saturates.
- **Ask**: everywhere, in context of main flows (after purchase, in onboarding, after inviting a friend). It shouldn't look like an ad. "Holidize" campaigns.
- **Target**: new users first (biggest cohort, untapped networks). Personalise amounts by user value or city.
- **Incentive**: extrinsic ($) beats points for outsiders. Tiered offers with breakage make a big headline number possible. Inviter-centric usually wins in consumer; B2B may favour altruistic framing. A/B test it.
- **Payback**: compare CAC/LTV with other channels. Measure **cannibalisation** with cost per incremental customer (twin-city test) or an on/off test.
Successful referrals = active users × participation % × invites/participant × invite→install % × install→engaged %.
Prefer evergreen **viral features** (share ETA, split bill, team invites, file sharing) over ever more complex referral schemes.

## Content loops (Casey Winters — Grubhub, Pinterest)
1. Get content from users. Reviews and geo pages are low-hanging fruit.
2. Give an incentive and a mechanism to share (YouTube embeds; support WhatsApp where it dominates).
3. Find where the community lives (Google, LinkedIn, HN).
4. Trace traffic by source and fix intent mismatch. Pinterest switched search users to topic-based onboarding: activation went up and the gender skew was fixed.
5. Convert and activate. **Friction is fine once value has been shown** (Pinterest's scroll-wall after a few pins).
SEO = relevance (enough text and metadata) + trust (links: local bloggers with promo codes, pitching your data as trend
stories). Build features for users that also help the bot (Grubhub reviews). Experiments are limited by
time-to-learn, not team size. Run them to learn, not to find silver bullets. **Never copy another company's growth
tactic**; it failed every time for Winters. Content compounds slowly (Auth0, Wistia: 2–3× faster growth per phase over years).

## Growth psychology (Currier, NFX)
Language first: the name and copy define the product ("get a ride in 4 minutes" vs "ridesharing marketplace").
Empathy for the user's whole life: what are you to them? (FB "see me perform", Snap "I'm sick of performing",
Instagram "see my good side", Etsy "feel unique", WeWork "belong"). Move constantly. Love data (put up to half of eng
on measurement). Sustain the pain of failure. Everyone owns growth. Take big swings; A/B tweaks alone won't 10× growth.

## Positioning
Why choose us over the alternatives? Research (needs × ability to pay) → segment → target the underserved segment →
position (message + visuals). Examples: Audi vs Maruti, iPhone (status, privacy) vs OnePlus (specs), Facebook
(connect) vs Snapchat ("fastest way to share a moment").

Compete to be **unique**, not best (Porter): markets are positive-sum, so several players can win different segments
(email marketing: Marketo for large companies, HubSpot mid-size, Mailchimp SMB). Without positioning, players fight on
incentives and burn cash (UPI apps). "The company without a strategy is willing to try anything." (Growth Catalyst)

## When the product isn't growing (Austin Chang — Pinterest, Google, Initialized)
Don't reach for tactics (funnel tweaks, SEO, push) before knowing who you build for. That buys a burst, then churn.
1. **Map one Critical User Journey**: one use case, a specific goal, with context (Pinterest personal style: browse → filter → curate → buy).
2. **Measure it**: skip vanity MAU early. Use one acquisition metric (signup + first action) and one engagement metric (repeat use by new users); together they give the activation rate. Make activation specific (Google Assistant: ≥1 successful query/day on a given surface, country and feature within 2 weeks).
3. **Pick product levers**: movable, measurable links between projects and the top-line metric. If top-line = L7 engagement and lever = actions per user, keep only projects that raise actions per user and cut the rest.
4. **Don't stack features or flows "to see what sticks"**. Stacked activation flows turned one product into a Rube Goldberg machine; collapsing to one path tied to one lever raised conversion.
5. **Let your most engaged users show the way**: take the most engaged cohort, look backwards at what they did on day 1, week 1 and month 1, then push new users toward those actions first and build engagement loops around them.

## Activation and onboarding
- Aha moment = the action set that separates retained users from churned ones. Express it as "action X times within Y days": FB 7 friends/10 days, Dropbox 1 file in 1 folder on 1 device, Instagram 10 followers/7 days, Slack 3 of 7 days active.
- Optimise onboarding toward the aha. Consider **flipping the funnel** so users get value before signup (Duolingo: lesson before signup ≈ +20% DAU; Airbnb browse before signup).
- Onboarding teardown checklist (BeReal case): who it's for and market size; competitive differentiation; when onboarding is "complete"; which metric the PM optimises in onboarding/FTUE; why each design element exists; what data is collected and why; improvements tied to that metric.

## Growth fundamentals

### Two ways to grow, two lines to grow
- **Only two ways to grow anything: users and usage** (more customers, or existing ones doing more). Spot growth
  questions by the verb (increase / reduce).
- **Top line** = customers × ARPU. Customers = new (more reach, broader value prop) + repeat (service quality). ARPU
  rises with higher price points, bigger baskets, delivery and platform fees.
- **Bottom line** = revenue − costs (marketing, sales, operations, admin).
- **Profit pools**: revenue with near-zero marginal cost that rides existing distribution. Ads (Flipkart ~₹5,000 crore
  a year, ~30% of revenue) and private labels. **Ads need distribution first.** Every ad slot has a **notional
  real-estate cost** (could a recommendation there earn more?), so test it. Ad friction is tolerated in proportion to how
  essential the product is; never compromise core value.
- **Feature growth = users of the feature × average usage per user.**

### Stages and why companies fail
- Founder hunch → research → MVP (problem–solution fit) → iterations/experiments → PMF → scale. Early stages test value;
  then feasibility; at scale, viability and scalability. PM work differs by stage (seed: ambiguity and strategy churn;
  Series A: audience, channels, unit economics; Series B+: improve, diversify, expand).
- **Three failure reasons**: no demand (most common), the product doesn't solve the demanded problem, channels fail to
  reach the demand.
- **Validate demand cheaply**: search volume (Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs), Reddit conversations (GummySearch).
  "Birthday wishes" ≈ 1.9M searches/month in India with weak results = validated demand.
- **Timing kills good ideas** (quick commerce in 2017; fantasy sports took ~8 years to hockey-stick). PMF is lagging; you
  can't create demand artificially.

### Minimum viable *test* (not product)
Three elements: **value proposition** (the promise to a segment), **riskiest hypothesis** (what would break the model;
test it first), **atomic unit** (the smallest thing you sell first: Amazon books, PhonePe UPI, one cohort course).
Test types: concierge (humans deliver the service), Wizard of Oz (product UI, human behind it), pre-sales (Tesla,
Kickstarter), explainer video (Dropbox, Sora), landing page + waitlist, piecemeal (stitch Zoom + Discord + Slack), single
feature (Paytm wallet). "The user does not care about your product, only whether the problem gets solved."

### Objective PMF test (maps onto Balfour's four fits)
**Repeatable, predictable, month-on-month growth with positive unit economics.** Repeatable = market–product fit;
predictable = product–channel fit; month-on-month = model–market fit; positive unit economics = channel–model fit. A
single spike that falls back isn't PMF.
- Market–product: core value prop + **time to value** ("the shorter the time to value, the higher the adoption") +
  stickiness. **Category creators** (latent needs) need channel-led category creation (influencers).
- Product–channel: early channels (founder network, cold outreach, influencers, PR, events) vs mature ones (SEO, paid,
  sales, virality, partnerships, content, community). **Be first on a new platform** (Zynga on Facebook; today ChatGPT,
  quick-commerce shelves, ONDC). The channel owns the rules (Google's helpful-content update, LinkedIn's algorithm).
- Channel–model quadrants: low ARPU needs low CAC (LinkedIn consumer, organic and ad-funded); high ARPU affords high CAC
  (LinkedIn Talent Solutions, demos and account managers). FrontRow (₹200–500 ARPU + heavy ads) bled.
- Model–market arithmetic: revenue target ÷ ARPU = users needed ($200M at $1 = 100M users). Realistic share 5–30%.
  Avoid shrinking markets.
- **Scale after PMF** via market expansion (cities, countries), segment/need expansion (food → grocery), channel
  expansion (organic → ads → partnerships → influencers).

### Product-led growth
The product does acquisition, activation, retention and expansion itself. Atlassian: no sales team until $100M revenue;
Jira only delivers value with teammates, so users pull in others. **Zoom** beat WebEx by being self-serve with a free
plan: users distribute meeting links, and the 40-minute cap converts payers because "nine out of 10 meetings run over".
Canva: watermarks and locked templates. **Product-led vs sales-led**: same funnel, but users experience value before
paying. **Consumer first for brand, enterprise for dollars.** Bow-tie model: narrow to the core action, widen through
monetise/retain/expand.

### Loops and network effects
Three primary loops: viral (LinkedIn contact sync, "sent via Hotmail"), paid (reinvest revenue into ads), content
(reviews indexed by Google → new buyers → more reviews: an SEO loop at zero CAC). Two-sided loops become network
effects (Uber). **Prioritise loops from the growth equation**: outcome → decision factors (reviews from real alumni) →
friction (can't find them) → the loop that removes it (e.g. an edtech "learn in public" loop).

### Referrals: the case-debrief lessons (GameOn)
- **Define success first**: a referred user who plays ≥1 game. Tree: referrers × invites per referrer × visits per invite
  × downloads per visit × first plays per download. Know what influences each node: who *can* refer (eligibility rule),
  incentive, share copy ("can you beat my score?"), store page (usually 15–30% conversion), first-game value prop.
- **Referral cycle time** (share → referee's first action) is the missed lever. Virality is speed, not just count
  (COVID's R). Link expiry compresses decisions.
- **Incentives buy adoption, not habit**, and aren't defensible (UPI cashback burn vs Duolingo's streaks with no money).
  People share for validation, identity, achievement and showing off (NFX "Why People Share"). Design with **game
  dynamics** (compete, collaborate, collect, progress, surprise) before **mechanics** (points, badges, leaderboards).
  One- vs two-sided, monetary vs non-monetary incentives are product decisions.
- Pair the KPI with a guardrail (CAC/LTV), run a fraud pre-mortem ("incentives always drive the wrong side of some
  humans"), and give both actors (referrer and referee) an incentive and an action. Many first referral
  launches fail.

### Getting a feature adopted
"Your users do not care about your product features." Non-users → casual users via **off-product awareness** (email,
influencers, account managers, PR) and **on-product awareness** (banners, coach marks, toasts, prime real estate,
first-try incentives); casual → power via incentives, comms and social proof; keep power users with loyalty and added
value. **Message the benefit, not what you built.** Infrequent-use products (tax filing) need top-of-mind recall first.
Features chasing the same outcome cannibalise each other. **Double down on signs of life** instead of hopping between
small launches.

### User states, retention and engagement
- **States**: new → activated or dormant/churned; activated → engaged or dormant; dormant → engaged (resurrection). Ask
  why users fail each transition.
- Journey: set up for value → **aha moment** (first Zepto order arrives in <10 minutes) → habit → **Eureka moment**
  (reflecting on time saved).
- **Retention is an output** of acquiring the right segment, keeping the promise on first use, engagement and
  resurrection.
- **Retention (breadth: how many come back) ≠ engagement (depth: how much they do).** Interviews often confuse them.
- **Hooked** (Eyal): trigger → action → **variable reward** → investment (Snapchat and Duolingo streaks; variable rewards
  beat predictable coupons). **Fogg**: behaviour = motivation × ability × trigger; a trigger only works when both are high
  enough.
- Growth stack at a large e-commerce company (PM-owned areas): push/email/in-app/SMS, FTUE, accounts, notifications, loyalty,
  community/content, lifecycle, gamification, assisted shopping, conversion optimisation, merchandising and ads,
  cross-sell, on top of analytics and tech infrastructure. "Write it as an equation; that's the best starting point."

### Positioning and moats
- **Positioning vs USP**: test positioning by asking a friend what makes the product unique. **Beachhead then expand**
  (Instagram → artists; Facebook → college students). Younger generations migrate away from their parents' platforms;
  adoption is social ("monkey see, monkey do").
- **Moats for low-differentiation products**: brand and trust, distribution, reliability and ease (GPay), rewards (CRED).
  Marketplace leakage (pros taking customers offline) is fought with value only the platform gives: training, dispute
  handling, payout control, safety features.
- Food-delivery economics: ~70% of the delivery fee goes to riders, leaving ~₹15–20 per order, hence platform fees.

## Go-to-market (GTM interview and real launches)
- **Goal first**: **adoption** (Instagram Reels) vs **top-of-mind awareness** (Zomato's quirky notifications and PR).
  **GTM solves discovery, adoption and resurrection, not retention.** "You typically get only that one chance."
- **GTM = target segment + channel + message.** Assume the feature is built; don't redesign it in a GTM answer.
- **Clarify**: what exactly is the feature, is placement decided, adoption vs availability, budget (decides channels),
  size of the bet (decides A/B or not).
- **Pre-launch, attention**: in-app touch points vs TV vs social by budget, time, audience and importance; comms (push,
  WhatsApp; **email works for B2B, poorly for consumers**; WhatsApp brand messages get ignored); PR, influencers,
  freemium. Selective, invite-only or geo rollout (5–20% first).
- **Pre-launch, execution**: A/B test the touch point (CTR → same-session conversion). Big conscious bets skip A/B (CRED
  Garage's vintage-car stunt).
- **Post-launch**: dashboards, feedback, **fast follow** on bugs and complaints, conclude the A/B.
- **GTM metrics**: % of existing MAU who try it in week 1 / month 1 (should be high, >50–60%); new users whose first
  action was the feature; lagging cohort retention vs pre-launch (attributable only through A/B).
- **Placement**: habitual touch points are sacred; moving one can cut its usage 30–50% (Paytm's UPI Pay button). The
  homepage PM and design team own placement and settle conflicts with data. Expect the follow-up "what do you displace?";
  answer with business importance and accepted short-term dips.
- Kill vs delay: a 2-day build with bad metrics gets killed; a 6-month build gets diagnosed first.
